# Parse Command — Problem Statement

Status: Initial working draft · 2026-11-04

## Background

Warfare commanders disseminate operational orders through dense, formatted telecommunications messages in USMTF format. Watchstanders must read hundreds of lines to determine when their ship is scheduled for events and what tasking applies to their command.

## Problem

Watchstanders and shipboard leaders need to determine which tasking, events, and correspondence apply to specified commands, when action is required, and which officer within Command C2 should coordinate it. Relevant information includes frequency plans, crypto change-over windows, and flight quarters event times.

The current workflow requires watchstanders to locate and interpret this information across dense message text. A consolidated understanding of the command's obligations depends on that manual interpretation. The frequency of missed tasking, interpretation errors, and the time required to assemble the schedule have not yet been established.

## Why This Is Worth Solving

Understanding command-specific obligations and their timing is directly relevant to watchstanders and shipboard leaders carrying out operational orders. The supplied challenge identifies the burden of reading hundreds of lines to determine a ship's scheduled events.

The team has representative messages and access to watchstanders or subject-matter experts who can validate interpretations. These resources provide a basis for testing the problem framing and evaluating results; validation findings have not yet been collected in this document.

## Why Now

Parse Command is developing a real operational capability. The immediate milestone is a proof of concept ready after two days at the hackathon. That deadline sets the initial validation window; the intended product extends beyond the hackathon presentation.
